Dear Dr.\ Alvarado,

Thank you for your assessment of our earlier submission (PATTERNS-D-26-00307) and for
inviting a revised, expanded version. We resubmit as a new manuscript implementing your
central recommendation: to consolidate the OmniGene-4 and BioPAWS work into a single,
balanced study with a fair benchmarking framework.

You were not fully convinced the paper offered a fair benchmarking framework, ``particularly
given that BioPAWS was also developed by your group.'' We took this seriously and addressed
it head-on. We have turned the benchmark into a first-class, openly released contribution ---
\textbf{BioPAWS-2}, a chat-form suite of 9 task families / 22 tasks ($\sim$306K examples),
released on Hugging Face (\texttt{dnagpt/biopaws-2}) with all code on GitHub, so any group
can score any model on it. Crucially, we then used our own benchmark to argue \emph{against}
a self-serving conclusion: a matched raw-Gemma-base control under identical joint fine-tuning
shows our model's biological continued-pretraining confers \emph{no material advantage under
supervised fine-tuning} (macro 0.769 vs.\ 0.760) --- the opposite of self-benchmarking.
OmniGene-4's value is instead in deployment cost ($\sim$1.5 GPU-days, 4-bit GGUF on a single
24\,GB GPU), modality breadth, and zero-shot remote-homology detection. We also release an
MMseqs2 leakage audit and re-split every sequence task entity-disjoint (homology leakage
0.78$\to$0.08); re-running all supervised results on the clean splits moves the headline by
only $-1.4$ points, showing the leaderboard is not a memorization artifact.

The empirical core of the original submission (router-level CPT-vs-SFT decomposition,
remote-homology results, modality-invariant transfer) is retained in full. All artefacts ---
dataset, model checkpoints, and training/evaluation/leakage-audit code --- are public
(\texttt{github.com/maris205/omnigene4}), so any leaderboard number is independently
reproducible. A preliminary version is on bioRxiv (DOI \texttt{10.1101/2026.01.03.697478}).
The author declares no competing interests; GPU compute was donated by io.net, which
exercised no influence over the research.

We are grateful for the path you offered toward a more balanced paper and welcome any further
feedback.
