Dear Dr.\ Alvarado,

Thank you for your considered assessment of our earlier submission
(PATTERNS-D-26-00307) and for the invitation to submit a revised and expanded version. We
are resubmitting as a new manuscript that directly implements your central recommendation:
to \textbf{consolidate the OmniGene-4 and BioPAWS work into a single, balanced study with a
fair benchmarking framework}. We summarize below exactly how this version addresses the
concern you raised.

\paragraph{Your concern.}
You wrote that you were not fully convinced the paper presented a sufficiently fair
benchmarking framework, ``particularly given that BioPAWS was also developed by your
group,'' and you suggested an integrated manuscript consolidating the two efforts. We agree
this was the right diagnosis: in the previous version, the central model was evaluated
largely on a benchmark from the same group that was neither independently described nor
released as a standing resource, which understandably reads as self-benchmarking.

\paragraph{What we changed.}
We have turned the benchmark itself into a first-class, openly released contribution and
re-positioned OmniGene-4-MM as \emph{one of several models evaluated on it}, rather than
its author-evaluator. Concretely:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{BioPAWS-2, a public benchmark and training resource (new Section~2).} We
    consolidate and substantially expand the benchmark into \textbf{BioPAWS-2}: a
    chat-form instruction-tuning suite spanning \textbf{nine task families and 22 tasks
    ($\sim$306K examples)} --- pairwise homology, protein function, DNA/genomic
    classification, variant effect, structure, cross-modal DNA$\leftrightarrow$protein,
    chain-of-thought reasoning, biomedical QA, and multimodal image+text. It is released
    openly on Hugging Face (\texttt{dnagpt/biopaws-2}) with all conversion and evaluation
    code on GitHub, so that \emph{any} group can train and score \emph{any} model on it.

    \item \textbf{A fair, paradigm-neutral evaluation protocol.} Every task is scored under
    two modes: zero-shot QA (for generative LLMs) and fine-tune-then-evaluate, where a
    specialized protein/DNA language model runs its \emph{native} frozen-backbone
    head-training protocol and a generalist LLM runs LoRA-SFT --- on the same train/test
    split, each in its home-turf regime. This lets a specialist PLM (e.g.\ ESM-2-3B) and a
    generalist LLM be compared on a common axis without disadvantaging either.

    \item \textbf{OmniGene-4-MM is one entrant among several.} The leaderboard reports
    OmniGene-4-MM alongside ESM-2-3B (per-task heads) and Qwen-3.7-max (zero-shot), across
    all families. Where the specialist is stronger (structure, localization) we report it
    plainly; the contribution is that a single generalist covers all tasks with one
    training run while remaining competitive, not that our model wins every cell.

    \item \textbf{New, independent findings that strengthen rather than assume the result.}
    Consolidation surfaced results we could not have shown in the narrower framing: (a) on
    ProteinGym variant-effect prediction, a mean-pooled PLM head sits at chance (0.53)
    because single-point-mutation signal is averaged away, whereas the chat formulation
    --- presenting the mutation as explicit tokens --- recovers it (0.67 after SFT); and
    (b) fine-grained biological label sets (protein fold, localization) are near-zero
    zero-shot for \emph{every} large model tested, quantifying precisely what the BioPAWS-2
    training corpus contributes.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What is unchanged.}
The empirical core of the original submission --- the router-level CPT-vs-SFT
decomposition, the remote-homology results, and the modality-invariant-transfer analysis of
OmniGene-4-MM --- is retained in full (Sections~3--5). The new BioPAWS-2 section provides
the independent benchmarking foundation those results now rest on.

\paragraph{Openness.}
All artefacts are public: the BioPAWS-2 dataset (\texttt{dnagpt/biopaws-2}), the model
checkpoints (\texttt{dnagpt/OmniGene-4-MM-merged} and the LoRA adapter), and all training,
conversion, evaluation, and leaderboard code (\texttt{github.com/maris205/omnigene4}). A
third party can reproduce every leaderboard number, or add a new model, without any
artefact private to our group.

\paragraph{Preprint and prior consideration.}
A preliminary version is on bioRxiv (DOI \texttt{10.1101/2026.01.03.697478}); an updated v2
with the multi-modal extension is at DOI \texttt{10.64898/2026.05.12.724542}. The present
manuscript adds the consolidated BioPAWS-2 benchmark and its independent evaluation. The
author declares no competing interests; GPU compute was donated by io.net, which exercised
no influence over the research.

We are grateful for the path you offered toward a more compelling and balanced paper, and
we believe this integrated version answers it. We welcome any further feedback.
